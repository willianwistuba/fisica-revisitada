Introduction

Before proposing new stabilization mechanisms, it is advisable to verify whether the simulator reproduces the classical criteria. This note reports that verification for three families of configurations: the Lagrange triangle with unequal masses 1,2, rings with a dominant central body 4, and hierarchical systems 6. It adds the effect of dissipation on gyroscopic stability 7 and documents islands of resonant instability within the linearly stable region 3. The simulation receives no information about the criteria; the thresholds emerge from direct integration.

Method

Units with \(G = 1\); leapfrog integration with a fixed step of 0.002, or an adaptive step proportional to \(r_{\min}^{3/2}\) in cases with close encounters; five perturbations per configuration, from \(10^{-4}\) to \(10^{-3}\) in position or velocity; integration limit between 600 and 3000 time units, depending on the family. A configuration is recorded as stable when no perturbation disrupts it within the limit, according to explicit geometric criteria for each family: relative deformation of the sides above 30%, angular deviation between neighbors above 30% of the spacing, or disruption of the inner binary.

Results

Lagrange triangle: the Gascheau-Routh limit

For masses \(m_1, m_2, m_3\) in a rigidly rotating equilateral configuration, linear stability requires 1,2

\[ (m_1 + m_2 + m_3)^2 > 27\,(m_1 m_2 + m_2 m_3 + m_3 m_1) . \]

With masses \(1 : \mu : \mu\), the limit is \(\mu \approx 0.0198\); that is, the dominant body must have more than about 50 times the mass of each of the others. The simulation reproduces the limit: all perturbations remain bounded up to \(\mu = 0.0195\), with a maximum deformation of 3.9%; at \(\mu = 0.0205\) the deformation grows slowly to 18.5% without disruption within the integrated interval; from \(\mu = 0.022\) onward all perturbations disrupt the triangle, with a median survival time that falls from about 60 time units at \(\mu = 0.022\) to about 8 at \(\mu = 1\).

Islands of resonant instability

The Routh condition is linear. Within the linearly stable region, the two eigenfrequencies of small oscillations about the equilateral configuration, in the rotating frame, are given by

\[ s^2 = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 27\beta}}{2}, \qquad \beta = \frac{m_1 m_2 + m_2 m_3 + m_3 m_1}{(m_1 + m_2 + m_3)^2} . \]

When the ratio \(s_1/s_2\) is a small integer, nonlinear terms can transfer energy between the modes. The scan with masses \(1 : \mu : \mu\) found exactly this: at \(\mu = 0.0122\) and \(\mu = 0.0124\), where \(s_1/s_2 = 2.02\) and \(1.996\), the larger perturbations (\(10^{-3}\)) disrupted the triangle, whereas at \(\mu = 0.0118\) and \(\mu = 0.0126\), with ratios 2.075 and 1.970, all perturbations remained bounded. With masses \(1 : \mu : 0.25\mu\), the same 2:1 resonance appeared at \(\mu = 0.020\) (\(\beta \approx 0.0239\)), within a range that the Routh criterion classifies as stable up to \(\mu \approx 0.032\).

The result is consistent with the classical nonlinear analysis of the restricted problem, in which the 2:1 resonance between the libration frequencies occurs at \(\mu \approx 0.0243\) 3; in terms of \(\beta\), the simulated value (\(\beta \approx 0.0237\) to \(0.0239\)) coincides. The 3:1 resonance, predicted near \(\mu \approx 0.0068\) in this family, did not manifest itself at the amplitudes tested.

Dissipation and gyroscopic stability

The triangular Lagrange points are maxima of the effective potential in the rotating frame. Their stability, when it exists, is gyroscopic: the Coriolis force converts the displacement into motion around the point. The Kelvin-Tait-Chetaev theorem states that the gyroscopic stability of a potential maximum does not survive dissipation 7. To verify this, a linear drag \(-\gamma(\mathbf{v} - \boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{r})\) was applied to the smaller bodies of a stable triangle with \(\mu = 0.01\). Discussion

In all stable families, the system decomposes into approximately Keplerian subsystems, and the coupling between them remains below a threshold. The separation of scales comes from mass (Routh), from distance (hierarchy), or from number and symmetry with a dominant mass (Maxwell). Within the stable regions, resonances between eigenfrequencies open islands of instability, and dissipation destroys stability of gyroscopic origin.

The main limitations are the planar geometry, circular orbits, the small number of perturbations per point, and finite integration times, which differ between families. The thresholds should be read as reproductions of known criteria, which validate the simulator, and not as new results. The exception is the numerical location of the 2:1 resonant island in the \(1 : \mu : 0.25\mu\) family, which we did not find tabulated in the literature consulted.

Conclusions

The simulator reproduces, without prior knowledge of them, the criteria of Gascheau and Routh, of Maxwell, and of Mardling and Aarseth, and confirms the Kelvin-Tait-Chetaev theorem. The observed regularity suggests a common heuristic, namely that stability depends on a small coupling between Keplerian subsystems. The next note in the series tests whether these thresholds can be described by a single coupling number.
